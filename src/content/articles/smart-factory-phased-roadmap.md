## The bottleneck is discovery bandwidth, not technology choice

When multiple work-streams — MES, WMS, APS, robotics/AGV, RPA — run in parallel under a single client sponsor, the constraint that actually determines how fast the program moves is that one named contact's availability, not which technology gets selected. Discovery intensity has to be explicitly sequenced and prioritized. Treating every work-stream as equally urgent doesn't speed any of them up; it dilutes the sponsor's attention until all of them stall a little.

## Validate the roadmap per factory, not once for the whole network

A digital-transformation roadmap for a multi-factory manufacturer can't be assumed uniform across sites. Local operational nuances — a specific changeover sequence, a specific product-mix pattern — invalidate a single generic template applied network-wide. What looks like one roadmap slide in a steering deck needs, underneath it, a per-factory validation pass before anyone commits to a timeline.

## Screen RPA candidates against real criteria, not visibility

Every RPA candidate process should be screened against explicit criteria — rules-based, structured inputs and outputs, high volume, error-prone, and a stable underlying process/system — before being proposed for automation. It's tempting to automate the process that's most visible to leadership rather than the one that actually scores well against these criteria. The visible process and the well-suited process are not always the same process.

## Never let case studies masquerade as this client's data

Illustrative industry benchmarks and reference architectures are genuinely useful for framing a conversation, but they must be explicitly labeled as illustrative and never presented as if they were the client's own confirmed current-state figures. Conflating the two produces a proposal with claims nobody can actually verify against reality — and the client will eventually notice the gap between the deck and their own operations.

## Pricing follows discovery, not the other way around

Firm commercial commitment should follow the Requirement Definition (As-Is/To-Be) phase, not precede it. Proposing fixed costs before As-Is validation is a bet dressed up as an estimate, and it's the single fastest way to end up renegotiating scope and price six weeks into a program that hasn't even finished discovery yet.

## Guard against template contamination between clients

When proposal templates get reused across different client engagements — which happens constantly under deadline pressure — pricing or example data from a different, unrelated client can leak into the wrong document. This is a genuine data-integrity risk worth an explicit review step, not just professional embarrassment if caught late. I've seen it happen, and it's a much smaller problem caught in an internal review than caught by the client reading their own proposal.
